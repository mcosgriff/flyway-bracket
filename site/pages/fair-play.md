A player in Maine and a player in Colorado do not see the same birds. Some birds never go near Maine.
Some never go near Colorado.

They can still play in one league. The game does not ask anyone to find a bird they had no chance of
seeing.

Two words make this work: *expected* and *eligible*.

## Expected

A bird is "expected" in a county, in a given week, when birders there often report it at that time of
year.

The game uses checklists to judge this. A checklist is the list of birds one birder reports from one
outing.

A bird is expected when it is on at least 3 out of every 100 checklists from that county for that time of
year.

The answer is always yes or no. A bird is expected, or it is not.

## Eligible

You are "eligible" for a bird in a round when you could have seen it.

That means the bird is expected that week in one of these places:

- your home county, or
- a county where you logged an outing that week.

## Why this is fair

A matchup counts only the eligible players for each bird.

Say a bird is expected in Colorado but not in Maine. The player in Colorado is eligible for it. The player
in Maine is not.

If the Maine player does not log that bird, the bird is not hurt. The Maine player was never counted for
it.

So a bird does not lose because it skips where some players live. It is judged only by the players who
had a real chance.

[How the game works](how-the-game-works.html) has an example with numbers.

## Trips count

Say you live in Maine and visit a friend in Colorado. You log an outing there.

For that week, you are eligible for the birds expected in that Colorado county as well. They are added to
the birds expected at home.

A trip can only add birds. You never lose your home county's birds by going somewhere else.

To log birds on a trip, download the pack for that state before you go. [Get started](get-started.html)
says how.

## States and counties

Bird information is downloaded one state at a time. But the game judges by county, not by state.

A big state can have very different birds from one end to the other. Counties are a closer fit to what
you can see near home.
