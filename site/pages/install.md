There is no release to install yet. When the beta starts, this is how it will work.

## What you need

You need an Android phone with Android 10 or later.

To see your version, open the phone's Settings, then "About phone".

Flyway Bracket will not be in the Play Store during the beta. You will get it from the project's
[Releases page](https://github.com/mcosgriff/flyway-bracket/releases) on GitHub. GitHub is the website
where the app's code is kept.

There are two ways to install it.

## With Obtainium

[Obtainium](https://obtainium.imranr.dev/) is a free app. It installs apps straight from their Releases
pages. It also tells you when a new version is out.

This is the easier way to stay up to date.

1. Install Obtainium. Its website says how.
2. Open Obtainium and tap "Add app".
3. Type this address as the source: `https://github.com/mcosgriff/flyway-bracket`
4. Tap "Add", then "Install".

## By hand

You can also download the app file yourself. An Android app file is called an APK.

1. On your phone, open the [Releases page](https://github.com/mcosgriff/flyway-bracket/releases).
2. Find the newest release. Under "Assets", tap the file whose name ends in `.apk`.
3. When the download ends, open the file.
4. Tap "Install".

With this way, the app does not update itself. Come back to the Releases page for each new version.

## The warning from Android

Android will warn you the first time. It may say the file can harm your phone. It may also say your
browser, or Obtainium, is not allowed to install apps.

This warning is normal. Android shows it for every app that does not come from the Play Store. It does
not mean anything is wrong with this app.

It does mean that nobody at the Play Store has looked at the app for you. So install an app this way only
when you trust where it comes from. Download Flyway Bracket only from the Releases page above.

To go on, tap "Settings" in the warning. Turn on "Allow from this source". Then go back and tap "Install".

## What the app asks for

Today the app asks for one permission: your approximate location, the first time you start an outing, to
find the county you are in. Saying no is fine. Your location never leaves your phone and is not kept.

That will change as the app grows. [Your privacy](privacy.html) says what is planned and why.
